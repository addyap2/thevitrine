Domain name, hosting, DNS, updates: these words come up as soon as you talk about a website, and people don't always like to ask what they mean. Here's the essentials, without the jargon.

## The domain name: your address

The domain name is your website's address: *wallstreetfrejus.fr* for [Wall Street](/en/work/wall-street-frejus/), *anglaisadistance.fr* for [Anglais à Distance](/en/work/anglais-a-distance/). It's also what your customers will remember and type.

A few tips for choosing one:

- **Short and easy to spell out** over the phone, ideally without hyphens.
- **Your name or your trade**, perhaps with your town.
- **Which ending?** A country ending (.fr, .co.uk, .it) reassures local customers and tells Google which country you're aiming at; .com suits an international audience. Some endings have rules: .fr, for example, is reserved for people and businesses established in the European Union and a few neighbouring countries.

A domain name is rented by the year, from a registrar. We use **OVH**, a French provider. If you already have a domain, we simply connect it to your new site.

One piece of advice, whoever builds your site: **know whose name your domain is registered in**, and note its renewal date. A forgotten domain that expires means a website that disappears.

## Hosting: where your website lives

Your website is a set of files that have to be served, day and night, to every visitor. That's the host's job. We host our sites on **Vercel**, which serves pages from servers spread around the world, so the site loads quickly whether your visitor is in Fréjus, Bristol or Milan.

What matters to you:

- **speed**, especially on phones;
- **the padlock (HTTPS)**, which secures the connection and which browsers now expect;
- **reliability**: a site that always answers.

## How the two connect

The domain name and the hosting are two separate services, linked by a few technical settings (the "DNS"). We take care of that: there's nothing for you to set up.

## Updates: who changes what?

A website is never quite finished: new hours, a new menu, a new service. There are two ways to handle it, depending on what you'd like:

- **you update it yourself**: your text, hours or photos;
- **you leave the updates to us**, as part of a subscription: you send a message, we do the rest.

And when the site needs to grow more, a new page or a new language, you call on us. We agree all this together for each project, before launch.
