A club or association lives on its members, its volunteers and the people who might become one. Often everything happens in a WhatsApp group or on a Facebook page, and newcomers don't know where to start. A simple website changes that.

## What people come looking for

Visitors to a club's website nearly always want the same things:

- **What's next**: the next match, the next big night, the next outing.
- **The calendar** for the season or the months ahead.
- **How to take part**: join, sign up, come and try, volunteer.
- **How to get there**: address, opening hours, directions.
- **Who to contact.**

If those five answers are visible within seconds, the site has done the essentials.

## Put what's coming up first

On the [Filton Athletic FC](/en/work/filton-athletic-fc/) site in Bristol, the next match is on the home page, with date, kick-off time and opponent, followed by the calendar and latest results. On the [Filton & District Social Club](/en/work/filton-district-social-club/) site, the next big night comes first, then the programme month by month.

In both cases, what changes most often is what you see first.

## Thank the people who back you

Partners and sponsors keep a lot of clubs going. Giving them a real place on the site, with their name and a link, is a simple way to say thank you, and one more reason for new ones to come on board. Filton Athletic FC scrolls all its sponsors in a banner across the top of the page.

## An identity that feels like you

Every club has a story. Filton's plays "where Concorde flew", and its site starts from there. A neighbourhood, a founding year, a tradition: those details are what make people want to come along.

## Plan for updates from day one

A club website is only useful if it's up to date. Before building it, ask what will change often (fixtures, results, events) and who will look after it. We build the site around that answer, so the information that moves is the easiest to keep current.
