In Fréjus and Saint-Raphaël, a restaurant lives with two audiences: year-round regulars, who want to know what's new this week, and summer visitors, who are discovering the town and often choose on their phone while walking along the seafront. Your website has to speak to both.

## In winter, give people a reason to come back

Out of season, your regulars keep the dining room going. They already know where you are: what they're after is what's new. Dish of the day, the week's menu, a live music night, a match on the big screen.

On the [Wall Street](/en/work/wall-street-frejus/) site in Fréjus, the week's menu scrolls across the top of the page, dish of the day by dish of the day, with the gig and match calendar right after it. The site becomes a reason to come back, not just an address.

## In summer, be understood in seconds

In season, searches look like this: "restaurant Fréjus", "burger Saint-Raphaël", "restaurant open now". Visitors compare three or four results on their phone, often on a patchy connection. They want to know straight away:

- **whether you're open**, today and right now;
- **what you serve**, and at what price;
- **where you are**, and how to get there;
- **how to book**, or order takeaway.

On [Mr. Tandoors](/en/work/mr-tandoors/), a live sign shows whether the restaurant is open, and the "Order takeaway" button is visible as soon as you arrive. [So Good Diner](/en/work/so-good-diner/), in the centre of Fréjus, shows its full menu, reviews and one-tap directions.

## Speak to visitors in their language

Some summer customers don't read French. An English version of your menu and practical details is often enough to win them over from the restaurant next door. Wall Street and So Good Diner both have English versions; we go into more detail in our article on [the tourist season](/en/blog/tourist-season-website-french-riviera/).

## Your Google listing and your site, together

For a restaurant, the Google listing is often the first contact: it's what appears on the map. But it doesn't show your atmosphere, your full menu or what's new this week. The website does that job, and the listing points to it. Make sure your hours, including summer hours and closing days, match on both.

## What to remember

1. Opening hours and closing day, visible on arrival.
2. The menu as text, not a PDF or photo.
3. What changes each week, at the top of the page.
4. A button to book, call or order, on every page.
5. An English version for the summer.
