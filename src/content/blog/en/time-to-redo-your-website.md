Your website has been around for a few years. It "works", but you're not really proud of it any more, and you're not sure it still brings in customers. Here are the signs that don't lie.

## 1. It's hard to read on a phone

Open it on your phone: do you have to zoom, scroll sideways, aim for tiny buttons? That's how most of your visitors see it. A site designed for computers first loses many of them.

## 2. It's slow

If the page takes several seconds to appear, some visitors leave before they've seen it. Heavy images, tools piled up over the years: it can often be fixed, though sometimes it's simpler to start again.

## 3. The information is out of date

Old opening hours, an old menu, services you no longer offer, photos from ten years ago. A visitor who finds one wrong detail starts wondering whether the rest is reliable.

## 4. You can't change it

The developer has vanished, the tool is too complicated, or every change costs a call-out. A website you can't update ends up looking its age.

## 5. It doesn't show up on Google

Search for your trade and your town: where are you? A site without clear titles, pages for each service or an address search engines can read stays invisible, however good it looks. Our article on [getting found on Google by people nearby](/en/blog/getting-found-on-google-locally/) covers the basics.

## 6. The browser says "Not secure"

Without the padlock (HTTPS), browsers warn your visitors. Not the best way to welcome them.

## 7. Visitors don't know what to do

No visible call button, a form hidden at the bottom of a page, a phone number you can't tap: if visitors have to hunt for how to contact you, they'll go elsewhere.

## 8. It doesn't speak your customers' language

If some of your customers come from abroad, a site in one language shuts them out. See [our article on multilingual websites](/en/blog/multilingual-websites/).
