When someone's looking for a restaurant, they're often hungry, often on their phone, and usually comparing. Your website has a few seconds to answer their questions and make them want to come. Here's what really matters.

## 1. Opening hours and phone number, straight away

The first question is nearly always: "Is it open?" Show your hours and closing day on the home page, and make your number tappable.

On the [Ristorante da Lola](/en/work/ristorante-da-lola/) site, a bar always shows the hours, the closing day and the number to book. On [Mr. Tandoors](/en/work/mr-tandoors/), a live sign shows whether the restaurant is open right now.

## 2. The menu as text, not a PDF

A menu as a PDF or a photo is hard to read on a phone, and Google doesn't understand it well. A menu written as text, sorted by category with prices, reads easily with one thumb and helps your restaurant show up when someone searches for a particular dish.

If your prices change, say so simply: "price to be confirmed" beats a wrong price.

## 3. What changes each week, up front

Dish of the day, the week's menu, live music, big matches: these are good reasons to come back. The [Wall Street](/en/work/wall-street-frejus/) pub shows its weekly menu at the top of the page and its agenda right after. The site becomes a reason to return, not just an address.

## 4. Booking or ordering in one tap

Depending on how you work: a button to call, a booking link, WhatsApp, or takeaway ordering. What matters is that there are only one or two, clearly visible on every page.

## 5. Make people hungry

Photos of your food and your dining room, a few words about your cooking and your story. On the [So Good Diner](/en/work/so-good-diner/) site, four simple commitments (baker's bread, butcher's meat, homemade sauces, cooked in front of you) say in one line what makes it different.

## 6. Directions and languages

A directions link, your full written address, and if you welcome tourists, a version in their language. That's often what makes up a passing visitor's mind.

## 7. Fast on a phone

None of this helps if the page takes ages to load on a terrace with a patchy connection. Well-optimised images and a light page are part of the job.

## In short

A good restaurant website doesn't need to be big: it has to answer the right questions quickly and make people want to sit down. See [our restaurant sites](/en/#work), or [tell us about yours](/en/#contact).
