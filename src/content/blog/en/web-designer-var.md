Search for "web designer Var" and you'll find agencies, freelancers, do-it-yourself platforms and cut-price offers. They're hard to compare. Here's how to find your way, and the questions that avoid nasty surprises.

## Three ways to get a website built

- **Do it yourself**, on an online platform. Cheap at first, but everything rests on you: the words, the photos, search engines, updates. The result often looks like thousands of other sites.
- **A large agency.** Plenty of skills, but often several people to deal with, and a budget to match.
- **An independent designer or a small team.** You talk directly to the people building your site, and the site starts from your business.

There's no universal right answer: it depends on your time, your budget and what the site needs to do for you.

## What drives the price

The price depends mostly on the number of pages, the languages, the features (booking, ordering, quotes), the content to write and what's planned after launch. We go through each item in our article [how much does a website cost](/en/blog/how-much-does-a-website-cost/). A good quote lists them all, with no "extras" appearing later.

## Questions to ask before you sign

1. **Can I see live sites you've built?** Not mock-ups: real sites to open on your phone.
2. **Who will actually build my site, and who will I deal with?**
3. **Will the site be designed for phones first, and set up for Google?**
4. **Who writes the words?**
5. **Whose name will the domain be registered in?** It should belong to you, or at least be easy to transfer.
6. **Can I update the site myself? If not, how do updates work, and what do they cost?**
7. **What's the timescale, and what do you need from me to keep to it?**

## Warning signs

- No live sites to show, only screenshots.
- A very low price with no detail of what's included.
- A long commitment or compulsory subscription with no explanation.
- The domain registered in the provider's name, with no way to get it back.
