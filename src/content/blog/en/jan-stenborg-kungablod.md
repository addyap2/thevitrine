## A trilogy, three books, one reader journey

Jan's trilogy follows Erik of Birka: *Stridslarm* (2017), *Jagad* (2021) and now *Kungablod*. When a new book comes out, readers arrive with simple questions: what is it about, where does it fit in the series, where can I buy it or listen to it, and when can I meet the author?

[His website](/en/work/jan-stenborg/) answers those questions in that order:

- **the new novel first**: the home page is devoted to *Kungablod*, with the next book signing right below;
- **the trilogy book by book**: year, publisher, page count, synopsis, and links to buy or listen (Adlibris, Bokus, Storytel), with a free audio sample;
- **events that are easy to attend**: add to calendar, map, sharing and a poster to download;
- **Jan's story**, a blog and an FAQ, for readers who want to know the person behind the books.

## Two languages, one site

Jan writes in Swedish, and his readers are mostly in Sweden. But he lives abroad, and more and more people around him read in English. So the site works in [both languages](/en/blog/multilingual-websites/), with a language switch on every page. Each reader lands on a version they can read, and nobody has to hunt for a translation.

## A look that feels like the books

An author's website shouldn't look like a generic template. Jan's takes the dark and gold of his covers: the new cover is shown as a framed object, and the site's main sections are numbered in Roman numerals, like the chapters of a book. The tone is that of a saga, but what matters is never hidden: the next event and where to find the books.

## Writers, artists, independent creators

What works for a novelist works for anyone whose work needs to be found: an illustrator, a musician, a photographer. A clear site, in the right languages, with your latest news up front, does work that a social media page alone can't.
